7Legal guardrails (avoidance vs evasion)

Everything above is tax planning using provisions Parliament deliberately wrote. Crossing the line costs far more than the tax saved:

  • Tax Administration Act 28 of 2011, s222–223: understatement penalty from 25% (substantial understatement) up to 150% (intentional evasion) of the shortfall, plus interest (s187–189).
  • TAA s235: intentional evasion or false statements are criminal offences (fine or up to 5 years).
  • TAA s25, s29, s99: submit a full and true return; keep records for 5 years (logbooks, medical, s18A, RA certificates); SARS can generally assess up to 3 years after an original assessment, 5 years for self-assessments, with no limit for fraud.
  • TAA s226–230: if you have under-declared in the past, a voluntary disclosure before an audit starts can remove criminal prosecution and cut penalties.
  • Anti-avoidance: the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Income Tax Act 58 of 1962 s80A–80L) and, for VAT, s73 of the VAT Act 89 of 1991, can unwind schemes whose sole or main purpose is a tax benefit. Don't split income, back-date or “round-trip” assets just to cut tax.
  • TAA s240: anyone who advises others on tax or prepares returns for a fee must be a registered tax practitioner.
